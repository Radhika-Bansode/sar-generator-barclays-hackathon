MONEY LAUNDERING TYPOLOGIES - INDIA CONTEXT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

TYPOLOGY 1: STRUCTURING / SMURFING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Definition:
Breaking large transactions into smaller amounts to avoid reporting 
thresholds mandated under PMLA 2002.

Indian Regulatory Threshold:
- Cash transactions ≥ ₹10,00,000 require CTR (Cash Transaction Report)
- Series of transactions ≥ ₹10,00,000 in aggregate within a month

Red Flag Indicators:
✓ Multiple transactions just below ₹10 lakh threshold (₹9-9.9 lakhs)
✓ Pattern of deposits in amounts like ₹9,50,000 or ₹9,99,000
✓ Coordinated deposits across multiple branches on same day
✓ Use of multiple individuals to deposit funds (smurfing)
✓ Customer resistance when asked about transaction purpose
✓ Sudden change in transaction patterns near month-end

Example Pattern:
Customer makes 10 deposits over 15 days:
- Day 1: ₹9,80,000
- Day 3: ₹9,75,000
- Day 5: ₹9,90,000
(Total: ₹98,50,000 but each transaction avoids ₹10L CTR threshold)

FATF Reference: Recommendation 10, 11
FIU-IND Circular: FIU-IND/2011/003

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TYPOLOGY 2: RAPID FLOW-THROUGH / LAYERING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Definition:
Funds deposited and quickly withdrawn to obscure the audit trail and 
distance money from its illegal source.

Characteristics:
- Short time between deposits and withdrawals (24-72 hours)
- Minimal balance retention (<5% of total inflow)
- No legitimate business purpose for rapid movement
- Often involves cross-border wire transfers

Red Flag Indicators:
✓ Credits totaling ₹50 lakhs+ received within 5-10 days
✓ 90%+ of credited funds withdrawn within 48 hours
✓ Multiple incoming transfers immediately consolidated
✓ Outgoing transfers to different beneficiaries/jurisdictions
✓ Account used as pass-through rather than for storage
✓ Transaction velocity inconsistent with customer profile

Example Pattern:
Week 1: Receives ₹1 crore from 30 different accounts
Week 2: Transfers ₹98 lakhs to 5 overseas beneficiaries
Retention: Only ₹2 lakhs (2%) remains in account

Classic "Layering" Phase Indicators:
- Multiple jurisdictional hops
- Complex routing through intermediaries
- Use of multiple currency conversions
- Involvement of shell companies or trusts

FATF Reference: ML Stages - Layering Phase
FIU-IND Circular: FIU-IND/2013/007

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TYPOLOGY 3: COLLECTION ACCOUNT / FUNNEL ACCOUNT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Definition:
Single account receives funds from numerous unrelated sources, then 
consolidates and transfers to final destination.

Normal Business Context:
Legitimate businesses (e-commerce, payment gateways) may show similar 
patterns BUT with proper documentation and business rationale.

Suspicious Indicators:
✓ Receiving from 20+ unique sources in short period
✓ Sources have no apparent relationship to account holder
✓ Sources located across multiple states/countries
✓ Incoming amounts relatively uniform (suggesting coordination)
✓ Immediate consolidation and onward transfer
✓ Lack of retention for operational expenses
✓ Account holder's stated business doesn't justify pattern

Example Pattern:
Small shop owner (stated income ₹6 lakhs/year) receives:
- 47 credits from 47 different individuals
- Total: ₹50 lakhs in 7 days
- All senders previously unknown to account holder
- No invoices, contracts, or business documentation
- Funds immediately transferred abroad

Questions to Ask:
1. What is the business relationship with each sender?
2. Why so many unique sources?
3. What goods/services were provided to justify payments?
4. Why immediate transfer rather than retention?

FATF Reference: Red Flag #12 - Multiple Source Accounts
FIU-IND: Suspicious Transaction Indicators List

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TYPOLOGY 4: TRADE-BASED MONEY LAUNDERING (TBML)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Definition:
Misrepresentation of price, quantity, or quality of imports/exports 
to transfer value and obscure illicit origins.

Common TBML Techniques:

1. OVER-INVOICING OF EXPORTS
   Purpose: Move capital out of country
   Method: Export ₹10L worth goods, invoice at ₹1 crore
   Result: Excess funds sent abroad "legitimately"

2. UNDER-INVOICING OF IMPORTS
   Purpose: Reduce customs duties, move black money
   Method: Import ₹1 crore goods, invoice at ₹10L
   Result: Difference paid through hawala

3. MULTIPLE INVOICING
   Purpose: Justify multiple payments for same goods
   Method: Same shipment invoiced 2-3 times
   Result: Illegal funds appear as trade payments

4. PHANTOM SHIPPING
   Purpose: Create paper trail for non-existent goods
   Method: False bills of lading, fake invoices
   Result: Money moves without actual trade

Red Flag Indicators for Banks:
✓ Transaction amounts inconsistent with commodity prices
✓ Shipments to/from high-risk jurisdictions
✓ Involvement of shell companies or front businesses
✓ Frequent amendments to Letters of Credit
✓ Descriptions of goods vague or inconsistent
✓ Multiple payments for same shipment
✓ Trading in goods unrelated to customer's business

Indian Context - High Risk Sectors:
- Gems and jewelry (under-invoicing common)
- Electronics (over-invoicing for capital flight)
- Textiles (both over and under-invoicing)
- Scrap metal (valuation manipulation)

FATF Best Practices: TBML Risk Indicators (2020)
RBI Guidelines: Master Direction on KYC - Trade Finance

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TYPOLOGY 5: ROUND-TRIPPING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Definition:
Capital taken out of India and brought back as Foreign Direct Investment 
(FDI) to appear legitimate and access tax benefits.

Typical Structure:
Indian funds → Mauritius/Singapore shell company → Return as FDI to India

Red Flag Indicators:
✓ Large outward remittances to tax havens
✓ Subsequent FDI received from same jurisdictions
✓ Timing correlation between outflow and inflow
✓ Use of shell companies with no real operations
✓ Opaque ownership structures
✓ Transactions routed through multiple jurisdictions

FIU-IND Guidance: Special attention to transactions involving Mauritius, 
Singapore, Cayman Islands, British Virgin Islands (BVI).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TYPOLOGY 6: CASH-INTENSIVE BUSINESS EXPLOITATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

High-Risk Business Types:
- Restaurants and hotels
- Retail shops and supermarkets
- Entertainment venues (theaters, arcades)
- Wholesale commodity trading
- Real estate brokers
- Jewelers and bullion dealers
- Money changers and forex dealers

Suspicious Patterns:
✓ Cash deposits inconsistent with business size
✓ Revenues far exceeding industry benchmarks
✓ No proportional increase in operating expenses
✓ Frequent large cash deposits with no sales invoices
✓ Business claims growth but no expansion in operations

Example:
Small restaurant (20 seats) deposits ₹15 lakhs cash monthly
- Menu prices suggest ₹3-5 lakhs realistic revenue
- No documented catering or bulk orders
- No credit card or digital payment records
- Likely co-mingling legitimate and illegitimate cash

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TYPOLOGY 7: PLACEMENT THROUGH MULTIPLE ACCOUNTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Definition:
Using numerous bank accounts to fragment and place illicit cash, making 
detection and source-tracing difficult.

Pattern Recognition:
✓ Customer maintains 5+ accounts at different banks
✓ Frequent inter-account transfers with no clear purpose
✓ Accounts opened in quick succession (within weeks)
✓ Similar transaction patterns across all accounts
✓ Use of family members' or employees' accounts
✓ Deposits fragmented to stay below monitoring thresholds

Example Network:
Person A's 3 accounts + Spouse's 2 accounts + Parent's 1 account
All receive cash deposits of ₹8-9 lakhs each within same week
Total placement: ₹50+ lakhs fragmented to avoid aggregation detection

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
INDIA-SPECIFIC TYPOLOGIES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

HAWALA / HUNDI SYSTEM

Characteristics:
- Parallel banking system outside formal channels
- No physical money movement across borders
- Settlement through reciprocal arrangements
- Often used for terror financing

Bank Red Flags:
✓ Customer receives funds from unrelated parties with no explanation
✓ Payments to beneficiaries with no documented relationship
✓ Amounts match international currency rates (suggesting forex)
✓ Customer unable to provide satisfactory source of funds

BENAMI TRANSACTIONS

Definition (Prohibition of Benami Property Transactions Act, 1988):
Property held by one person on behalf of another to conceal true ownership.

Banking Indicators:
✓ Funds deposited by third parties into customer account
✓ Customer claims beneficial ownership by someone else
✓ Multiple individuals funding single account with no explanation
✓ Power of attorney usage to obscure real ownership
✓ Transactions claimed to be "on behalf of" unnamed parties

REAL ESTATE TRANSACTIONS

High-Risk Characteristics:
✓ Property purchases with large cash components
✓ Under-valuation in registration documents
✓ Multiple buyers/sellers for single property
✓ Quick flip sales (buy and resell within months)
✓ Use of power of attorney to hide beneficial owners
✓ Purchase price significantly below/above market rate

ELECTORAL FUNDING MISUSE

Suspicious Patterns:
✓ Large cash deposits around election periods
✓ Deposits in round numbers from multiple individuals
✓ Described as "political donations" without documentation
✓ Amounts inconsistent with donor's known income
✓ Use of multiple accounts to receive similar amounts

CROSS-BORDER SPECIFIC RED FLAGS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

High-Risk Jurisdictions (FATF Grey/Black List):
Current monitoring: Pakistan, Myanmar, Syria, Yemen, Iran, North Korea
(Check FATF website for updated list)

Transaction Red Flags:
✓ Wire transfers to/from sanctioned countries
✓ Routing through multiple jurisdictions unnecessarily
✓ Use of correspondent banks in tax havens
✓ Beneficiary in high-risk jurisdiction with no business ties
✓ Transaction purposes vague ("family support", "gift")
✓ Amounts structured to avoid SWIFT reporting thresholds

RBI Liberalized Remittance Scheme (LRS) Misuse:
- Annual limit: USD 250,000 per individual
- Suspicious: Multiple family members remitting maximum amounts
- Red flag: No clear purpose or investment documentation
- Concern: Coordinated capital flight

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
OCCUPATION-SPECIFIC RISK INDICATORS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

STUDENTS / UNEMPLOYED / RETIRED
Normal activity: Limited, low-value transactions
Red flags: 
✓ Receiving ₹10+ lakhs from multiple unknown sources
✓ Conducting transactions inconsistent with no income
✓ Foreign remittances not matching educational expenses

SMALL BUSINESS OWNERS
Normal activity: Regular receipts from known customers
Red flags:
✓ Revenue 5-10x beyond reasonable business capacity
✓ No corresponding inventory purchases or operating costs
✓ Cash deposits far exceeding card/digital payments
✓ Business activity inconsistent with location/size

SALARIED EMPLOYEES
Normal activity: Monthly salary credit, regular expenses
Red flags:
✓ Credits other than known employer salary
✓ Amounts exceeding annual salary significantly
✓ Regular cash deposits with no explanation
✓ Foreign remittances beyond known family ties

POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS (PEPs)
Enhanced scrutiny required per PMLA rules
Red flags:
✓ Wealth accumulation inconsistent with known income
✓ Use of relatives' accounts to obscure ownership
✓ Shell companies or trusts with opaque structures
✓ Transactions with high-risk jurisdictions

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
REGULATORY FRAMEWORK - INDIA
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Key Legislation:
1. Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002
2. Prevention of Money Laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005
3. Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA), 1967
4. Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999

FIU-IND Reporting Obligations:
- Cash Transaction Report (CTR): Transactions ≥ ₹10 lakhs
- Suspicious Transaction Report (STR): Any amount, any suspicion
- Cross Border Wire Transfer Report: All international transfers
- Counterfeit Currency Report: All instances

Filing Timeline:
- STR: Within 7 days of classification as suspicious
- CTR: Monthly (by 15th of following month)

Penalties for Non-Compliance:
- PMLA violations: Fine up to ₹1 lakh per day
- Willful non-reporting: Imprisonment up to 2 years
- Entity penalties: ₹10,000 to ₹1,00,000 per violation

FINCEN RED FLAGS - COMPREHENSIVE CHECKLIST
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CUSTOMER BEHAVIOR RED FLAGS:
✓ Customer reluctant to provide identification documents
✓ Customer is secretive about transaction purpose
✓ Customer makes unusual inquiries about reporting requirements
✓ Customer attempts to avoid recordkeeping requirements
✓ Customer opens multiple accounts without clear business purpose
✓ Customer shows unusual familiarity with AML regulations

TRANSACTION PATTERN RED FLAGS:
✓ Deposits just below ₹10 lakh CTR threshold (₹9-9.9 lakhs)
✓ Multiple deposits on same day totaling significant amount
✓ Cash deposits followed immediately by wire transfers
✓ Pattern of round-number transactions (₹1,00,000, ₹5,00,000)
✓ Transactions inconsistent with customer's known business
✓ Sudden increase in account activity without explanation

GEOGRAPHIC RED FLAGS:
✓ Transactions involving high-risk countries
✓ Wire transfers to/from tax havens
✓ Transfers to countries with weak AML controls
✓ Customer conducts business in locations unusual for their profile

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RISK ASSESSMENT MATRIX
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

LOW RISK (0-39 points):
- Continue normal monitoring
- Standard customer due diligence sufficient
- No immediate SAR filing required

MEDIUM RISK (40-69 points):
- Enhanced monitoring recommended
- Additional customer due diligence
- Consider SAR filing based on totality of circumstances
- Document decision-making process

HIGH RISK (70-100 points):
- IMMEDIATE SAR filing required
- Freeze account or restrict transactions if appropriate
- Enhanced due diligence mandatory
- Consider law enforcement notification
- Senior management notification required
